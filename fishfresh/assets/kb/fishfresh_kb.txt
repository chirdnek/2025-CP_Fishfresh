Q: What is the context of FishFresh?
A: FishFresh is developed for Zamboanga City, a major fishing hub in the Philippines. Freshness of fish is critical to nutrition, health, safety, and fair trade. Traditional freshness checks are subjective and inconsistent, leading to disputes and food safety risks.

❓ Why Freshness Matters

Q: Why does fish freshness matter?
A:

Fresh fish = safe, nutritious, tasty.

Spoiled fish risks illness.

Reliable freshness builds trust, reduces waste, and supports fair pricing.

⚠️ Problems with Traditional Checks

Q: What are the problems with traditional freshness checks?
A:

Based on subjective judgment (eyes, gills, skin, smell).

Leads to disagreement between buyers & sellers.

Many consumers lack the skill to check accurately.

Causes distrust, unfair prices, and safety risks.

✅ Solution

Q: What is FishFresh?
A:

A mobile app that uses computer vision + AI.

Analyzes fish photos (eyes, gills, skin, shine).

Gives a freshness score + label (Fresh / Not Fresh).

Works in real-time (3–5s) using a smartphone camera.

Helps both consumers and vendors by making decisions objective.

🎯 Objectives

Q: What is the main objective of FishFresh?
A: To develop and evaluate a mobile app that gives an objective, real-time freshness classification, replacing subjective judgment.

Q: What are the specific objectives?
A:

Assess freshness using visual cues (eye clarity, gill color, skin shine).

Build a mobile app for capturing & analyzing fish images.

Train and integrate a CNN model for ~3–5s inference.

Display a clear score and easy-to-understand result.

📱 Scope & Limitations

Q: What is the scope of FishFresh?
A:

Users: consumers and vendors in Zamboanga City.

Use case: point-of-sale (markets, groceries).

Features: image capture, AI-powered analysis, simple UI, profile & history.

Q: What are the limitations?
A:

No IoT hardware (mobile only).

Uses visual freshness only (not long-term storage).

Not for cut/chopped fish.

Trained only for common local species.

Accuracy may vary with camera quality & lighting.

🧪 Research Design

Q: What research design is used?
A:

Developmental–Experimental:

Developmental → build the app.

Experimental → test CNN models (MobileNetV2, EfficientNet-Lite, ResNet50).

Compare accuracy, speed, and efficiency.

Best-performing model → deployed in the app.

📊 Data Sources

Q: Who are the data sources?
A:

BFAR → provided freshness standards (national guidelines).

ZSCMST Experts → validated dataset (eyes, gills, skin, body firmness).

Fish Vendors → ~80 vendors supplied market fish samples.

Consumers → ~100 buyers surveyed for freshness-checking habits.

📸 Data Gathering

Q: How was data collected?
A:

Image Collection → photos from local markets (front + back view).

Expert Validation → fisheries specialists confirmed freshness categories.

Preprocessing → resize (224×224), normalize, augment (flip, zoom, contrast).

Documentation → store metadata (species, freshness, timestamp, location).

📈 Data Analysis

Q: How is data analyzed?
A:

Quantitative → stats, confusion matrix, regression on lighting/angles.

Qualitative → expert validation, consumer surveys.

Mixed Methods → triangulation for both scientific accuracy & usability.

🖥️ Software Development

Q: How is FishFresh developed?
A:

Frontend → Flutter (cross-platform).

Backend → Firebase (Auth, Firestore, Storage).

ML Model → MobileNetV2 (converted to TFLite for mobile).

Features:

Biometric login.

Scan history & profile.

Push notifications.

Two-step capture (front & back photos).

🔄 Development Approach

Q: What approach is used to develop FishFresh?
A:

Agile Methodology (short sprints, iterative improvements).

Feasibility checks:

Technical → can smartphones handle it?

Operational → will users adopt it?

Economic → affordable development & maintenance.

🗃️ Database Schema (Firestore)

Q: What does the database look like?
A:

users collection

userId, email, profile info, biometrics enabled.

assessments collection

assessmentId, userId, timestamp.

imageURL, freshnessScore, freshnessLabel, species.

🤖 AI Model

Q: What AI models are used?
A: MobileNetV2, EfficientNet-Lite, and ResNet50 are tested.

Q: What is the input and output of the model?
A:

Input → fish images (224×224).

Output →

Categorical: Fresh / Not Fresh.

Numerical: Freshness score (0–100).

Q: What are the requirements for the model?
A:

Accuracy ≥ 90%.

Inference ≤ 5 seconds.

Robustness against lighting and species variations.

🧪 Testing & Evaluation

Q: How is the system tested?
A:

Unit Testing → camera, AI, Firebase modules.

Integration Testing → modules working together.

System Testing → full app checks (real-time, robustness).

User Acceptance Testing (UAT) → vendors & consumers try it in real markets.

Q: What metrics are used to evaluate performance?
A: Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-score, confusion matrices, and user feedback.
